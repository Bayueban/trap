02 / DAY TRIPS AND DETOURS

The city is only
the beginning.

Within a few good hours: red cliffs, temple trails, ancient Shu artifacts, misty pools and enough reasons to keep the return date loose.

LESHAN

Carved into a cliff. Kept by the river.

DAY TRIP 01 / LESHAN

The Giant Buddha,
at river scale.

About 45 minutes by high-speed rail from Chengdu East, the Leshan Giant Buddha makes an easy full-day escape. The 71-metre seated Maitreya was carved directly into a red sandstone cliff during the Tang Dynasty, overlooking the meeting of three rivers.

Take the Nine-Bend Cliff Path for the close-up descent, or see the full figure from a river boat. Finish around Zhanggongqiao with qiaojiao beef, sweet-skin duck and boboji skewers. A weekday morning is the calmest time to go.

DAY TRIP 02 / MOUNT QINGCHENG

Where the forest
holds its breath.

Mount Qingcheng is one of Taoism's historic birthplaces and a gentle, practical day trip from Chengdu. Its evergreen ridges, old temples and drifting mist earned it a reputation for quiet long before the city grew around it.

Choose the Front Mountain for Taoist temples and layered history, or the Back Mountain for streams, waterfalls and a longer walk. A cable car up and an unhurried walk down gives you the best balance. End with gingko chicken stew and Qingcheng cured pork.

DAY TRIP 03 / MOUNT EMEI

A mountain made
for the summit.

Mount Emei rises to 3,079 metres and is one of China's four sacred Buddhist mountains. Aim for Golden Summit, where the Ten-Faced Samantabhadra statue looks over the cloud sea.

It is a slow climb through Buddhist history, thick forest and alpine weather, so leave time for Wannian Temple and the mountain's carved details before continuing upward.

The macaques are part of the route too: keep your food packed away, do not feed them, and give them space. Bring a warm layer even in summer; for sunrise, stay near Leidongping, set off before dawn and check the forecast and current transport arrangements the evening before.

ANOTHER CIVILIZATION / SANXINGDUI

Sanxingdui,
awake again.

Less than an hour from Chengdu, Sanxingdui introduces the ancient Shu kingdom through a visual language unlike anything else in China. The discoveries from its sacrificial pits reshaped how we understand the region's early civilizations.

Look for the Bronze Standing Figure, the fantastical masks and the Sacred Tree. The museum deserves more than a quick stop, so reserve ahead and give yourself time to move slowly through the galleries.

03 / TIBETAN PLATEAUS

West gets
very wide.

The road beyond Chengdu reaches the Tibetan cultural landscapes of western Sichuan. Travel with time, humility and a plan that can change with altitude and weather.

LARUNG GAR / SERTAR

A valley of
maroon roofs.

At roughly 4,000 metres, Larung Gar unfolds across a high plateau valley in Sertar. The scale of its red wooden homes is extraordinary, but this is a living religious community first, not a backdrop for a fast visit.

Allow several days from Chengdu, acclimatize carefully and confirm current access rules before departure. Follow local guidance, ask before taking photographs, and keep a generous distance from religious ceremonies and restricted areas.

TRAVEL
AS A
GUEST.

These are living communities, not a backdrop. Ask before photographing people or religious spaces, follow local guidance and keep your pace respectful.

LIANBAOYEZE / ABA

Stone castles,
glacial blue.

Near the Sichuan, Gansu and Qinghai border, Lianbaoyeze is a dramatic field of glacier-cut granite. More than 300 glacial lakes sit between its sharp ridges, including the blue waters of Zhagaercuo.

The road from Chengdu is long and the elevation rises from about 3,800 to 4,520 metres. Stay overnight in Aba County to acclimatize, enter early, and keep the afternoon flexible when fog rolls through the peaks.
